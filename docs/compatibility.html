    On a Mac?Mac support is coming soon, for iPad and iPhone Duo. Until then, EternalMonitor streams from Windows PCs, and Apple's Sidecar works with an iPad.

    Compatibility

    The PCs and iPads EternalMonitor has been tested on, what was measured, and what nobody has measured yet. Tried it on something else? Tell us how it went.

    What it needs

    • A 64-bit PC with Windows 10 version 1809 or later, or Windows 11. It has not been tested on Windows PCs with Arm processors, such as Snapdragon laptops.
    • NVIDIA, AMD or Intel graphics. Without a graphics card that can encode video, the processor does it and the picture may lag.
    • An iPad with iPadOS 17 or later.
    • For Wi-Fi, the PC and the iPad on the same network. Guest networks usually keep devices apart.
    • For USB, a cable that carries data and Apple's free Apple Devices app, open on the PC. Apple Devices needs Windows 10 version 22H2 (build 19045) or Windows 11.
    • For pressure, Apple Pencil (1st or 2nd generation) or Apple Pencil Pro. Apple Pencil (USB-C) has no pressure sensor.

    Source: the setup guide and known issues.

    Tested setups

    Every number below comes from one PC and one iPad, measured on September 23 and 24, 2026, on the code that became v0.3.0. The hardware verification record has each run, including the ones that failed. Releases 0.3.1 to 0.3.3 changed the encoders, the second screen and Apple Pencil, and their results are not published yet.

    PC
    Windows 11, AMD Ryzen 7 7800X3D with Radeon graphics, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5080 on driver 591.86, a 3440 × 1440 main display.
    iPad
    iPad Pro 12.9-inch (M2), iPadOS 26.2.
    Connection
    The PC's home Wi-Fi network, and a USB-C to USB-C cable in the PC's rear USB-C port.

    Result

    Conditions
    1,800 seconds as a second screen at the iPad's 2732 × 2048, on a build from just before Apple Pencil drawing was added.

    Known problems on some hardware

    • On laptops with two graphics chips, such as Intel with NVIDIA, the second screen may not start. Mirroring works. Details.
    • On some PCs the second screen starts at 800 × 600. Details.
    • Over a USB cable, older or slower PCs can stutter. Details.
    • NVIDIA cards can't encode screens wider than 4096 pixels, such as 5K monitors, so the processor does it. Details.
    • On the test PC, NVIDIA driver 591.86 crashed Windows once, two seconds after a switch to the second screen over USB. A retest of at least 22 switches did not repeat it. Details.
    • HEVC can glitch on some iPads and graphics cards. Details.

    Not measured yet

    None of these has a published result. If you have one of them, your report fills a gap.

    • Intel laptops. Release 0.3.1 fixed Intel hardware encoding. The Windows 10 PC with Intel Iris Xe graphics that reported the problem has not been retested.
    • AMD laptops. AMD encoding has only been measured on the test PC's Radeon graphics, in a desktop.
    • Laptops with two graphics chips. Release 0.3.3 changed how the second screen starts on them. No results yet.
    • Other iPads. Only the iPad Pro 12.9-inch (M2) has been measured. An iPad Pro (M5) is waiting for its first test. No older iPad has been measured.
    • Latency, USB against Wi-Fi. No delay has been measured on either connection. The planned test films both screens at 240 frames per second.
    • 120 frames per second. The PC and the iPad agree on 120, but no iPad with a 120 Hz screen has been shown to display it.
    • Apple Pencil in drawing apps. Pressure, tilt and palm rejection have not been measured with a real Pencil in Clip Studio Paint, Krita, Photoshop or any other app. Nor has the delay from Pencil to screen.
    • Cable changes. How fast USB takes over from Wi-Fi when you plug in, and Wi-Fi takes back over when you unplug.
    • Windows PCs with Arm processors. Not tested at all.

    Source: the hands-on checks in the hardware verification record, the release notes and known issues.
