    Use an iPad as a second monitor for Windows

    Windows can't use an iPad as a screen by itself, so you need an app on each. This guide uses EternalMonitor, which is free and open source. Setup takes about 10 minutes.

    What you need

    • A 64-bit PC with Windows 10 version 1809 or later, or Windows 11.
    • NVIDIA, AMD or Intel graphics. Without a graphics card that can encode video, the processor does it and the picture may lag.
    • An iPad with iPadOS 17 or later.
    • For Wi-Fi, the PC and the iPad on the same home or office network. Guest networks usually keep devices apart.
    • For a USB cable, one that carries data, and Apple's free Apple Devices app on the PC. Apple Devices needs Windows 10 version 22H2 (build 19045) or Windows 11.

    The compatibility page lists the PCs and iPads it has been tested on.

    Set it up

    1. Install EternalMonitor on the PC

    On the download page, click Download for Windows and open EternalMonitor-Setup.exe. The installer is not code-signed yet, so Windows may show Windows protected your PC. Click More info, then Run anyway. If Windows asks Would you like to install this device software?, click Install. That driver is what lets the iPad be a second screen.

    2. Install the iPad app

    The iPad app is a TestFlight beta, open to everyone. Install TestFlight from the App Store, open the EternalMonitor invite on the iPad, then tap Accept and Install.

    3. Connect

    Keep EternalMonitor open on the PC; it says Ready for your iPad. Open the app on the iPad and tap Allow when it asks to find devices on your local network. Then tap your PC under Found on this network, scan the QR code from the PC window, or type the Address the PC shows.

    4. Pair once

    The first time on Wi-Fi, type the six-digit Pairing code from the PC window and tap Pair iPad. Scanning the QR code skips this. After that the iPad remembers your PC.

    The setup guide has every step with the exact wording on screen.

    Extend or mirror

    At first the iPad mirrors your main screen. To make it a second screen, go to the Stream page in the PC window and, next to Use the iPad as, click Second screen. The iPad reconnects by itself. Drag a window past the edge of your main screen and it moves onto the iPad.

    Arrange screens, next to that choice, opens Windows' Display settings, where you drag the iPad to the side of your monitor it sits on. The second screen exists only while the iPad is connected.

    To mirror again, click Mirror my screen. With more than one monitor, pick the one to mirror in Settings, under How the iPad shows your PC. On the iPad, choose Picture: Fill in Settings to fill the screen and trim the edges, or Fit to see all of it with bars.

    Wi-Fi or USB

    Start with Wi-Fi. A cable gives a steadier picture and charges the iPad.

    For USB, open Apple Devices on the PC and leave it open; it can stay minimized. Plug in the iPad, unlock it, and tap Trust the first time. Open EternalMonitor on the iPad and it connects over the cable. USB needs no pairing code. If you start on Wi-Fi and plug in the cable, the app can switch to it.

    On the test PC, a 30-minute second screen over USB dropped no frames, and a 30-minute mirror over Wi-Fi dropped 19 of 105,600. A USB-C to USB-C cable charged the iPad; the PC's USB-A port supplied too little power. The compatibility page has the conditions.

    The stream is not encrypted. Pairing keeps other devices out, but use Wi-Fi you trust, such as your home network. Over a cable nothing goes over the network.

    Draw with Apple Pencil

    Apple Pencil works as a Windows pen, with pressure and tilt.

    • In the iPad app's settings, turn on Drawing mode, then reconnect. It ignores fingers on the canvas and mutes PC audio. A USB cable gives the smoothest strokes.
    • In your drawing app, pick the Windows pen setting. In Clip Studio Paint, choose Preferences, then Tablet, then Tablet PC.
    • Open the drawing app normally, not with Run as administrator, or Windows blocks the pen.
    • Apple Pencil (USB-C) has no pressure sensor, so its lines keep one width.

    If something goes wrong

    • The iPad finds no PC. Check that EternalMonitor is open on the PC and both are on the same Wi-Fi, not a guest network. Some networks keep devices apart; use a cable there.
    • The iPad says Your PC didn't answer. A VPN or virtual network on the PC can show the wrong address. Tap the PC under Found on this network instead. If Windows Firewall blocks it, run EternalMonitor-Setup.exe again.
    • USB never connects. Keep Apple Devices open, tap Trust on the iPad, and try a cable that carries data. Apple Devices needs a newer Windows than Wi-Fi does.
    • The second screen is small and blurry, or doesn't start. See small second screen and second screen doesn't start. On some laptops with two graphics chips, use Mirror my screen for now.
    • The picture stutters. Over USB on an older PC, set Frame rate to 30. On Wi-Fi, move closer to the router or use a 5 GHz network. More.
    • Apple Pencil doesn't vary the line. Check the Clip Studio Paint setting and your Pencil model.

    Every message the apps show, with what to do, is in the setup guide's If something goes wrong tables. The rest is on known issues.

    Questions

    Is EternalMonitor free?

    Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT license, with no account.

    Can Windows use an iPad as a monitor without an app?

    No. You need an app on the PC and one on the iPad. Apple's Sidecar does this only for a Mac.

    Does EternalMonitor work with a Mac?

    Not yet. Mac support is coming, for iPad and iPhone Duo. Until then, Sidecar works with a Mac and an iPad.

    Which iPads work?

    Any iPad with iPadOS 17 or later. So far it has been measured on an iPad Pro 12.9-inch (M2).

    Can two iPads connect at once?

    No. One iPad can connect to a PC at a time.

    Is the stream encrypted?

    Not yet. Pairing keeps other devices out, so use a network you trust. Over a cable, the stream stays on the cable.

    Does Apple Pencil pressure work?

    Yes, with tilt, as a Windows pen. Apple Pencil (USB-C) has no pressure sensor, so its lines keep one width.
